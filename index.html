Is your seed genuine?

Certified seed sold to farmers carries a coloured tag. Knowing the system helps you spot a problem before sowing, not after.

Breeder seed — golden yellow tag (not sold directly to farmers)
Foundation seed — white tag
Registered seed — purple tag
Certified seed — blue tag (what most farmers buy)
  • Buy only from a licensed dealer and always take a bill with the batch/lot number written on it.
  • Check the tag colour and lot number sealed to the bag — blue means certified seed.
  • Make sure the bag is sealed, not re-stitched, and check the germination % and purity % printed on the tag.
  • Check the packing date and validity period — don't buy old stock past its tested date.
  • Note the dealer's licence number from the bill in case you need to raise a complaint later.
  • Before sowing the full field, test a small sample: wrap 20–25 seeds in a damp cloth for a few days and count how many sprout.
  • If germination is poor or the crop looks abnormal, keep the bag, tag and bill as evidence.
  • Report suspected spurious seed to your local Agriculture Department, Krishi Vigyan Kendra, or the Kisan Call Centre on 1800-180-1551.

Safety precautions

General good practice — always follow the specific instructions that come with your machine or chemical label.

Machinery

  • Keep PTO shafts and moving parts covered with their guards at all times.
  • Switch off and remove the key before clearing a blockage or doing repairs.
  • Avoid loose clothing or dupattas near rotating parts.
  • Keep children well away from tractors, threshers and harvesters while running.

Spraying & chemicals

  • Wear gloves, a mask and full-sleeve clothing while mixing or spraying.
  • Spray early morning or evening, and never against the wind direction.
  • Wash hands, face and clothes thoroughly after spraying, before eating or smoking.
  • Store chemicals locked away from children, animals and food grain.

Seed & storage

  • Store seed and grain in a dry, well-ventilated, pest-free godown, off the ground.
  • Check stored bags periodically for insects, dampness or rodent damage.
  • Keep the storage area's rope, ladder and lifting equipment in good condition to prevent falls.

Weather

  • Stop fieldwork and move to shelter at the first sign of lightning or thunder.
  • During heatwaves, work early morning or evening and drink water often.
  • Check the weather section above before planning spraying, harvesting or transport days.
